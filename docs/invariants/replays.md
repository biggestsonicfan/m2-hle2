# Replays of online matches (`core/replay.h`, Pinboard #572)

Part of m2-hle2's load-bearing invariants, indexed in [CLAUDE.md](../../CLAUDE.md): facts reverse-engineered or debugged into the emulator, in no datasheet. Do not re-derive them.

A replay is follow.h's join point and feed for one match, zipped with a label: `replay.json` (players, characters, stage, date, winner, rounds, the room's settings), `state.sta` and `inputs.feed`, saved as `<p1>-vs-<p2>-<YYYYMMDD-HHMMSS>.m2replay` (~140 KB for a two-minute match). The desktop's Replays menu turns recording on (kept in `<user dir>/replay.cfg`, files in `<user dir>/replays`, `--replay-dir`), and opens one, shows its label and asks before it plays; the web page's Replay tab does the same and keeps them in IndexedDB. `--record-replays`, `--replay FILE` and the bridge's `replay` command do it from a script.

- **The snapshot is taken after the session reset has zeroed `total_steps`** (`replay_session_reset`, in `emu_netplay_pump` after the reset). The feed's frame check hashes it, so a snapshot from before the reset split every playback at frame 0.
- **Playback is a follower**: it installs the replay's profile, detaches the battery and puts both back on Stop. Anything that would touch the board meanwhile stands aside: the idle hold, a state load (refused), and on the web the PS3 shell (`web_shell_live`). A replay from another build plays exactly as long as the board is the same: a wasm page played a native recording to its result, every frame check passing.
- **One result per replay.** The profile's versus hook (`match_info`, `sfight_match_info`) ends the recording `REPLAY_TAIL_FRAMES` later. A VS rematch is the same cold boot, so the next match starts a new replay from a fresh snapshot of where the board is. PS3 cross-play matches are not recorded: our lockstep does not run their board.
- **GGPO matches are recorded too** (Pinboard #584): `emu_ggpo_start` begins one replay (names from the lobby, `"netcode":"ggpo"` in the label), and rollback rewinds the recorder with the board. Each GGPO save marks the recorder (feed length, tail countdown, last inputs) in a ring keyed by frame, and a load cuts the feed back to that mark; a load to before the replay began (a rematch that was resimulated) drops it and begins it again at the next slice. Only the last few predicted frames of the tail can disagree with the opponent's file, and they still play back as recorded; in a local test with `--ggpo-delay 0` (thousands of rollbacks) both sides wrote byte-identical files for six matches. Synctest is not recorded.
- **Replays are STF's own format by default, and play through the game's own replay code** (`native/round-NN/`, `profiles/sfight_native.h`, [NATIVE-REPLAY.md](NATIVE-REPLAY.md); Pinboard #602, #617). Each round keeps the key bank the game records into (`0x531000`, what attract's replay fight plays), its round-start snapshot (`0x572000`, `0x573000`) and the ending slot, taken at round end (`0xDD20`), plus five parts that make it whole: every frame's keys (the bank stops at 2048), `fighters.bin` (both robs, the COP's carried DM words, the motion blend RAM, `mod_fa_coli`/`obj0`/`camera`), the game assignments, `play_motion`'s `rand` draws and the camera's view toggle. Playback sends attract to its replay fight and feeds it round after round; ADV_REPLAY_DSP checks the energies against `round.json` ("as recorded"). `--replay-format inputs` (Replays → Save as) still saves #572's cold-boot state and inputs too; `--replay-view` picks how to play.
  - **`fighters.bin` is taken at rob 0's first task visit after `key_rec_disp`'s first frame, not at `key_rec_disp`.** In a game the key task runs before the robs; in attract's replay `key_play_disp` runs after them. Taken at `key_rec_disp`, every per-frame counter in the robs played a frame behind.
  - **The bank and its snapshot alone do not replay a round**: they split within frames. What else the fight reads was found by dumping both robs every frame under native playback and under the inputs follower of the same match, and diffing. Do it that way again for anything new.
- **A local game records until it goes back to attract, so the player can finish it** (Pinboard #861). Free play and continues can keep a game from ever reaching attract, and its replay from ever being saved. `replay_rec_finish_local` saves what is recorded so far ("finished by the player") and holds recording off until that game ends (`held_off`), so it does not restart on the next slice. On the web it is the record button left of Pause, shown while `web_replay_status().local`; on the desktop, Replays → Finish the replay; on the bridge, `{"cmd":"replay","finish":1}`. The usual drop rules still apply: a native-format replay with no finished round, or one under `REPLAY_MIN_FRAMES`, is not kept.
- *Testing it:* two headless clients on the container's RPCN, each with its own `--net-config` and **no** `--net-p2p-port` (they step to free ports), started with the bridge's `netplay_start`. Play a replay back with `--replay FILE` and `{"cmd":"replay"}` until `playback.ended`; `split` names the first frame that left the record.
- **A game with no replay format of its own always saves its inputs** (Pinboard #877). `replay__saves_inputs` is true when the profile has no `native_take`, whatever `--replay-format` says: otherwise every replay would be "empty" and dropped.
- **Sega Rally's own format is each course's clip** (Pinboard #997, `profiles/srallyc_native.h`, NATIVE-REPLAY.md "Sega Rally"). A round is a race, taken at the championship's race end (`0x5BAE40`); it plays back through the championship ending's credits, 679 frames a course, sent there from attract at the mode dispatch (`0x5A2200`). Practice (sub-state 7, #1002) has no race end: the dispatch takes its clip on the first frame its race state (5) is left, a round marked `"practice":1` that plays alone. Sega Rally's `in_play` is the main mode byte `0x202098 == 3`, from the coin-up's select screens to game over (`profiles/srallyc.h` has the RAM map). Its `match_info` says `players = 1`: replay.json gets `"players":1`, the car as `p1_character`, the course as `stage` (the last one raced), no rounds and no winner, and the file is `srallyc-<date>-<time>.m2replay`. The wheel stays deterministic: the recorder latches `g_input.use_net`, so `io5649_wheel` uses the spec's adjustment while recording and while following, and a playback on a board with `--wheel-center`/`--wheel-range` set played a recording to its end, every frame check passing. A replay is about 1.5 MB (the state is most of it).
- **Virtual-On saves its inputs too** (Pinboard #995). Its own replay is only the KO replay: `Replay` (ROM 0x87AC0) plays a 2-second ring that the recorder at 0x87F60 refills every round (each robot's whole 0x600-byte `PL1EV`/`PL2EV` every 4th frame, 30 slots from 0x51D5F0 and 0x5289F0), so no format of the game holds a round, let alone a match. The attract demo is CPU against CPU (`Demoplay`, `set_ai_buf`), not a replay. `vonj_in_play` is `MainMode` (0x5039F4) 3 or 4, from the coin-up's select to game over; `vonj_match_info` reads `machine_P`/`machine_E` (0x503A98/9C, the ROM's name order TEMJIN..Z-GRADT), `FieldNo` (0x5770F0, no names yet), `SetWin`/`SetLose`, `NumOfSets` + 1, and calls a round on at `SubMode` (0x503A00) 10. `VersusMode` (0x503A7C) 0 is against the CPU. The RAM names are the PS3 build's symbol table (rokuYAMP `docs/von-rom-symbols.md`). A one-round loss to the CPU recorded 6270 frames and played back to the same loss and back to attract.
